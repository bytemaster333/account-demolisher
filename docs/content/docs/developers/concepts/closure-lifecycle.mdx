---
title: Closure lifecycle
description: The four stages a close moves through, and what each one guarantees.
---

A close is a sequence of ordinary Stellar transactions. The app assembles the right ones in the
right order, grounds them in live network state before you sign, and recovers when that state
shifts underneath.

Four stages: **discover**, **plan**, **preview**, **execute**.

## 1. Discover

Horizon is read for the full account state. Soroban RPC is scanned for the account's SEP-41
approvals, and each one is read back from the token's `allowance()`; those that read zero are
dropped. Blend, Aquarius and Soroswap are probed for open positions, the Aquarius reward gauges
for what they owe, and the account's transfer history for standalone tokens.

The audit, the reads of positions that keep the account from merging, and the payout trustline
checks are required: a failure there fails discovery or blocks the close. The allowance and
standalone-token scans are best effort, and an incomplete scan is reported as a warning with how
much it covered.

[Account audit](/docs/developers/internals/account-audit) ·
[Position discovery](/docs/developers/internals/position-discovery)

## 2. Plan

The audit, the positions and the allowances feed a pure generator that emits a directed acyclic
graph of steps. There are 20 node kinds, and a node is emitted only when the account needs it.

Each node lists its dependencies. A Blend withdraw waits for that pool's repays, a repay waits for
the exits that can fund it, an exit that pays into a new trustline waits for that trustline, and
the classic close waits for every other node, so the merge never runs while a position is open.
When a Blend backstop withdrawal stays locked after the run, the plan has no merge at all and only
prepares the close.

The graph is validated when built: duplicate ids, missing dependency targets and cycles are
rejected.

[The plan graph](/docs/developers/internals/plan-graph) ·
[The classic batch](/docs/developers/internals/classic-batch)

## 3. Preview

Each Soroban step is built and run through the RPC's `simulateTransaction`, and its resource fee,
authorization entries and footprint are assembled into the transaction. The sign-time checks then
run on every required step: an allow-listed root contract, exactly the authorization tree the call
needs, and a fee under the cap for its kind of step. A step that fails any of this is marked failed
with its reason, and confirming is disabled while any required step is failed.

A step that runs after other steps and cannot be built against today's state (a collateral
withdraw behind its repay, a sweep behind the exits that pay it) is deferred: it is built and
simulated at execute time once those steps have landed.

The classic close is not RPC-simulated. Its batches are built locally from the audit, and the
preview checks mergeability against the claims the plan really makes.

[Simulation](/docs/developers/internals/simulation)

## 4. Execute

Execution walks the graph in a stable topological order. Each Soroban step is rebuilt from fresh
state, checked again, signed through the connector, submitted and confirmed before its dependents
run. The classic close is rebuilt from a fresh audit before every phase.

You sign once per transaction, because Stellar does not combine Soroban and classic operations in
one transaction.

[Execution and recovery](/docs/developers/internals/execution)

## What holds across the stages

**The merge never runs early.** Before each classic phase the executor audits the account again,
discovers DeFi positions again and refuses while any is open or a protocol could not be read,
checks that no exit payout token is still on the account, and checks mergeability against what the
batch really claims.

**A step that did not land blocks its dependents.** A dependent of a failed step is skipped, never
run around it. Only a best-effort step (an allowance revoke, a standalone token sweep or
conversion) that was skipped on purpose lets its dependents run.

**Nothing is reported done until it confirmed.** A node is complete only when it carries the
receipt of its own transaction. The classic close records its receipt only when the merge batch
lands, and an exchange close succeeds only when the forward out of the forwarding account lands.

**A retry re-runs what did not land.** A retryable failure runs the same reviewed plan again: steps
with a receipt are skipped and every other step is reset and runs again.
