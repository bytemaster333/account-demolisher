---
title: Close a shared account
description: Collect signatures from the signers of a multisig account and submit the close once the threshold is met.
icon: Users
---

When the key you connect cannot meet the account's signing threshold on its own, the close
needs signatures from other signers. The app detects this from the account's signers and
thresholds and adds a **Signatures** step after Review, where you collect them.

Every signer signs on their own device. Nobody shares a secret key, and the app relays only
the transaction and its signatures, which are public data.

## Run it

<Steps>

<Step>

### Start the close as normal

Connect your key and fill in Configure. A **Shared account** bar shows the signing weight the
close needs and how many signers that takes.

On a shared account nothing is sold on the market. Every asset other than XLM is returned to
its issuer or sent to the destination, as you choose in the **Fix issues** step. A market sale
can fail when the price moves, and one failed operation would sink the single transaction
everyone signs.

</Step>

<Step>

### Create the signing request

At the bottom of Review, select **Create signing request**. The app builds the reviewed close
as one transaction, signs it with your key, and publishes it to the signing relay.

Nothing is submitted to the network yet. The account stays open until enough signers sign.

</Step>

<Step>

### Share the link

The Signatures step shows a link under **SEND TO THE OTHER SIGNERS**. Select **Copy link** and
send it to your co-signers. Each of them opens it and signs from their own device. See
[Sign a closure request](/docs/users/sign-a-closure-request) for what they see.

</Step>

<Step>

### Watch signatures arrive

The **Signing weight collected** bar updates as each signature arrives. Below it, every signer
on the account is listed with its weight and a **Signed** or **Pending** badge. Your own key is
marked **You**.

Updates stream live. When the stream drops, the page falls back to polling the relay and shows a
**Live updates interrupted** notice with a **Refresh** button that pulls the latest signatures.

</Step>

<Step>

### Close it

Once the collected weight reaches the threshold, select **Continue to final confirmation**. The
app checks the request against the live account first, then opens the same typed confirmation
as any other close: type the last four characters of the destination, wait for the timer, and
confirm.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Signing several keys yourself

If you hold more than one signer key, you do not need the link for those keys. Under **SIGN A
KEY LOCALLY**, paste a key and sign. Each signature moves the bar up. The keys are used in your
browser only and are never uploaded or stored.

## What a shared close cannot include

A signing request collects signatures on exactly one transaction, so the whole close has to fit
in one classic transaction. When one of these applies, the app refuses the signing request and
names the reason, before you start or on the review:

| Situation                                                                    | Why it does not fit                                                           | What to do                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The destination is an exchange                                               | The exchange path runs as several transactions                                | Close to an address you control instead                                                             |
| The account holds Soroban DeFi positions                                     | Each exit is its own transaction                                              | Unwind them first, or close with all signers present                                                |
| The account has token allowances to revoke                                   | Each revoke is its own transaction                                            | Revoke them first, or close with all signers present                                                |
| You selected standalone tokens to sweep                                      | Each token move is its own transaction                                        | Leave them out, or close with all signers present                                                   |
| The account holds a classic liquidity pool share                             | The pool withdraw runs in its own transaction first                           | Withdraw it first, or close with all signers present                                                |
| You chose to claim a claimable balance in a token the account does not trust | The trustline is opened and the claim lands in a transaction of its own first | Leave it unclaimed, or close with all signers present                                               |
| You chose to claim a claimable balance in a token with no disposal chosen    | The claim has to land in its own transaction first                            | Choose to send it or return it to its issuer, leave it unclaimed, or close with all signers present |

The close itself also has to fit in 100 operations, the Stellar limit for one transaction. The
review refuses a close over that and gives the count.

## How long you have

The bundled transaction is valid for 72 hours. The relay serves the request until the earlier of
72 hours after you publish it and one minute before the transaction's own expiry. After that you
create a new request.

The relay keeps requests in the server's memory, so a server restart drops them. When the relay no
longer has your request, the notice offers **Publish this request again**. The link stays the same
and every signature collected in your browser is kept.

## Before the close is submitted

When you confirm, the app checks the collected transaction against the account as it is now. It
refuses to submit when the account changed in a way that makes the transaction fail (its
sequence number, balances, offers or signers), when a DeFi position appeared since the request
was built, or when the collected signatures no longer reach the threshold of the current signers.
In each case you build a new signing request and every signer signs again.

When the network asks a higher fee than the request bid, the app offers a fee bump paid by your
connected wallet. The signatures cover the inner transaction only, so nobody has to sign again.
