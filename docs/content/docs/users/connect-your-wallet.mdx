The app needs to know which account you are closing and how you approve each transaction. You
choose both on the first screen.

## Pick the network first

The network selector sits in the top right of every page and offers **TESTNET** and **MAINNET**.
Your choice is remembered in your browser. A fresh visit starts on the network the deployment was
built for, which is testnet on [demolisher.app](https://demolisher.app).

Switching to mainnet opens a dialog titled **Switch to Mainnet?**. Tick the acknowledgement that
the tool has not completed an independent security audit, then select **Switch to Mainnet**. When
the app opens on mainnet in a browser that has not acknowledged this yet, from a mainnet build or a
remembered mainnet choice, the dialog **You're on Mainnet** asks for the same acknowledgement or
offers **Switch to Testnet**. The acknowledgement is remembered in your browser. Switching back to
testnet applies at once.

<Callout type="warn">
  Changing the network disconnects your wallet. A connected account belongs to one network, so the
  app ends the session rather than carrying it across. Connect again after the switch. The selector
  is locked while a close is running.
</Callout>

## Choose how you sign

### Connect a wallet

Select **Connect wallet** to open the wallet picker. It lists Albedo, Freighter, Fordefi, Rabet,
xBull, LOBSTR, Hana Wallet, Klever Wallet, OneKey Wallet, Bitget Wallet, Cactus Link, D'CENT Wallet,
Scopuly, MetaMask and GHOSTSIG, plus Ledger for hardware signing. WalletConnect appears as well when
the deployment sets a WalletConnect project id, and lets a mobile wallet connect by QR code.
Wallets you do not have installed stay in the list with an install link.

Your wallet keeps your secret key. The app receives your public address, builds each transaction,
and hands it to your wallet to sign. After the wallet signs, the app checks that the signed
transaction is the one it built and that the signature is from your key before it submits it.

### Use a Ledger

Ledger signs over WebUSB. Connect the device, unlock it, and open the Stellar app on it before you
select Ledger. The device shows the full transaction, operations and destination included, and you
approve it there. The app never asks a Ledger to sign a bare transaction hash.

When a transaction is too large for the device to show, the Ledger refuses it and the app tells you
to close the account with another wallet. The app does not fall back to hash signing.

### Paste a secret key

If you have no wallet installed, open **Advanced: paste a secret key** on the connect screen, enter
a key starting with `S`, and select **Continue with secret key**.

The key stays in your browser's memory for as long as that connection lasts, and the app signs in
the page with it. It is never written to storage, never sent to any server, and never copied to
your clipboard. Disconnecting drops the app's reference to it.

<Callout type="warn">
  A wallet is safer. Anyone who has your secret key controls the account. Only paste a key on a
  device you trust, with nobody able to see your screen.
</Callout>

### Create a demo account

On testnet the connect screen also offers **Try it on a demo account**, which builds a funded
practice account for you. See [Try it on testnet](/docs/users/try-it-on-testnet).

## What the app can see and send

Connecting shares your public address. That is enough to read your account from the network and
build a plan. The app never receives your secret key on the wallet path and never transmits it on
the secret-key path.

Your browser talks to these services directly:

- **Horizon and Soroban RPC** for the selected network, to read the account and submit
  transactions.
- **The Aquarius API**, to find your Aquarius pool positions.
- **WalletConnect**, only when you pick WalletConnect, sign with a WalletConnect session you
  restored, or disconnect or close such a session. Its relay carries the encrypted session, and
  its RPC, API and verify hosts back the connection and the Reown AppKit modal. The app turns
  AppKit's analytics and the WalletConnect client's telemetry off. AppKit still tries to send two
  events it always reports, the initialization and a successful connection, to
  `pulse.walletconnect.org`, and your browser blocks them, because the app's Content Security
  Policy leaves that host out.

The app's own server receives first-party usage counts: which page you opened, which network you
are on, four milestones of a close (wallet connected, discovery done, plan built, close started),
and the public transaction hash of a finished close, which the server verifies on chain before it
counts it. No account address, wallet name or amount is sent, and the server stores no IP address
with these counts.
