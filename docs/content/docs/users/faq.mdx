## Is my secret key sent anywhere?

No. With a wallet, the wallet holds your key and signs each transaction itself. With the
secret-key option, the key stays in your browser's memory for as long as that connection lasts,
and the app signs with it in the page. It is never stored, sent to a server, or copied to your
clipboard.

## What does it cost?

The app is free and takes no cut. You pay the Stellar network fees of the transactions it submits.
Review shows an estimate and a maximum before you sign.

## Can I close an account that holds DeFi positions?

Yes. The close unwinds Blend, Aquarius and Soroswap positions before it merges the account, and the
plan shows each exit step first. A Blend loan you cannot repay from the account and a Blend backstop
deposit need action from you. See [Close an account that holds DeFi](/docs/users/close-with-defi).

## What happened to FxDAO support?

FxDAO shut down on chain: its mainnet vaults contract only allows eviction and upgrades and holds
no vaults, and its testnet contracts no longer exist. There are no FxDAO positions left to unwind,
so the app no longer looks for them. FxDAO stablecoins you hold are ordinary balances the close
sells or disposes of like any other.

## Why can't my account be closed?

Two conditions stop a close outright: the `auth_immutable` flag is set, or the account sponsors
entries for other accounts. Others block it until you act, such as a balance frozen by its issuer.
See [Clear a blocked closure](/docs/users/clear-a-blocked-closure).

## What happens to a Blend backstop deposit?

Withdrawing one runs through a queue of 17 days on the current backstop and 21 days on the earlier
one. The close queues it and runs every other exit, and the account stays open to receive the
withdrawal. On or after the unlock date, run the close again: it withdraws the deposit and closes
the account.

## How many times do I sign?

Once per transaction. Stellar does not combine Soroban and classic operations in one transaction,
so every DeFi step and every allowance revoke is its own signature, plus the classic close. The
classic close is one transaction unless it holds over 100 operations or the account has a classic
liquidity pool share, which is withdrawn in a transaction of its own first. An exchange close's
final delivery needs no signature from you.

## Can I send straight to an exchange?

Yes. Exchanges credit payments with a memo, not account merges, so for a known exchange address,
or any destination that declares a memo requirement, the app routes the funds through a temporary
forwarding account that pays the exchange with your memo. See
[Send the funds to an exchange](/docs/users/send-to-an-exchange).

## What happens if a step fails partway through?

Steps that landed stay on chain. Depending on how far the close got, the account is still open, is
closed, or its balance sits in the temporary forwarding account of an exchange close. The failure
screen says which, and **Retry** either runs the remaining steps or rebuilds the plan. See
[When a step fails](/docs/users/when-a-step-fails).

## Can I close someone else's account?

No. Transactions are signed by the wallet you connect. Without the account's key, or enough
signatures to meet its threshold, nothing signs. For a multisig account you control, see
[Close a shared account](/docs/users/close-a-shared-account).

## Does it run on mainnet?

Yes. The same code runs on mainnet and testnet. The network selector in the top navigation switches
between them, and your choice is remembered in your browser. A fresh visit to
[demolisher.app](https://demolisher.app) starts on testnet. The first time a browser works on
mainnet, the app asks you to acknowledge that it has not completed an independent security audit.

## Can I get my account back after closing it?

No. Closing deletes the account from the ledger. Use **Copy a receipt** or **Download .txt** on the
success screen to keep a record, because a closed account cannot be looked up afterwards.

## Do I need to unwind positions before I start?

No. Connect the account and the plan comes back with the exit steps in it.

## What if I hold a token nobody recognizes?

A standalone SEP-41 token appears in the **Standalone tokens** card of the Warnings step, where you
choose whether to sweep it. If it stands for a position in a DeFi app the close does not support,
unwind it in that app first, because moving the token as-is does not reclaim what it represents.
