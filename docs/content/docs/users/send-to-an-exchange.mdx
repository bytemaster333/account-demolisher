You can send a closed account's balance directly to an exchange deposit address. The app
handles the part that makes a plain close to an exchange go uncredited.

## Why this needs special handling

Closing an account on Stellar ends with an account merge. Exchanges share one deposit address
across their customers and credit deposits by the memo on a payment. A merge is not a payment,
so a merge straight into an exchange lands in the exchange's wallet with nothing that ties it to
you.

When the destination needs a memo, the app routes the close through a temporary forwarding
account created for this one close. Your account merges into it, and the forwarding account then
pays the exchange in XLM with your memo and merges itself into the same address. From the
exchange's side the payment is an ordinary memo-bearing deposit.

The app takes this route when the destination is one of the exchange addresses it knows, or when
the destination account declares that it requires a memo (the `config.memo_required` data entry
from SEP-29).

## Do this

<Steps>

<Step>

### Get your deposit address and memo from the exchange

Copy both from the exchange's XLM deposit page. The memo is the part that tells the exchange the
deposit is yours.

</Step>

<Step>

### Paste the address into the destination field

When the app knows the address, a notice names the exchange and the memo types it takes.

</Step>

<Step>

### Set the memo type and value

Pick the type the exchange asked for and paste the value. **Continue** stays disabled while the
memo is missing, of a type the exchange does not take, or malformed for its type.

</Step>

<Step>

### Continue as normal

The rest is the standard flow in [Close an account](/docs/users/close-an-account). The plan gains
a last step, **Deliver to destination**: the forwarding account paying the exchange. That step
needs no signature from you.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What arrives at the exchange

The close funds the forwarding account with 2 XLM in the same transaction that merges your account
into it. The forward then pays the exchange everything above the forwarding account's own minimum
balance and the forward's fee, with your memo, and merges the forwarding account into the exchange
address. The merge carries the rest, about 1 XLM, with no payment and no memo, so count on the
exchange crediting the payment only.

Only XLM reaches an exchange. Every other balance is sold for XLM first, or, where it has no
market, returned to its issuer. Sending a token as-is is not offered, because it would reach the
exchange without your memo.

## Exchanges the app knows

Thirteen deposit addresses are built in, and all of them require a memo.

| Exchange          | Memo type  |
| ----------------- | ---------- |
| Kraken            | Text       |
| Binance           | ID         |
| Binance Deposits  | ID         |
| Bitfinex          | Text       |
| Bitstamp          | ID         |
| KuCoin            | Text       |
| Upbit             | Text       |
| Upbit Deposits    | ID or Text |
| CEX.IO            | Text       |
| Crypto.com        | Text       |
| Robinhood         | Text       |
| Coinbase Deposits | Text       |
| Blockchain.com    | Text       |

Kraken publishes a 1 XLM minimum deposit and Binance a 0.1 XLM minimum. Check your exchange's own
deposit page for its current terms before you close.

Upbit shows its deposit address only to logged-in customers. The two Upbit entries come from the
public stellar.expert directory, which tags both as exchange accounts that require a memo.
Seven other Upbit accounts in that directory are cold storage and withdrawal wallets, not deposit
addresses, so the app refuses them as a destination.

## An exchange that is not listed

When the destination account declares a memo requirement under SEP-29, the app takes the
forwarding route for it too and accepts any well-formed memo.

When it declares nothing, the close is a direct merge, and a memo you enter rides on the merge
transaction. An exchange that credits only payments does not credit that. Withdraw to a wallet you
control instead and send the funds to the exchange from there as a normal payment.

If you run an exchange and want closes to reach your deposit addresses, see
[Exchange integration](/docs/developers/protocol/exchange-integration).

## Things worth knowing

**Standalone tokens cannot go to an exchange.** An exchange deposit address cannot receive a
Soroban token. The app tells you to move any you want to keep to a personal wallet first;
otherwise they stay on the closed account.

**DeFi payouts have to be sellable.** An exchange close forwards XLM only, so the plan refuses a
DeFi exit whose payout cannot become a classic balance the close sells, such as the LP token a
Blend backstop withdrawal pays. Close to your own address in that case.

**A shared account cannot close to an exchange.** The exchange route runs as several transactions,
and a multisig signing request collects signatures on exactly one. See
[Close a shared account](/docs/users/close-a-shared-account).

**A Blend backstop still locked.** When a backstop withdrawal stays locked after the run, the run
only prepares the close: it creates no forwarding account and sends nothing to the exchange. Come
back after the unlock date to run the close to the exchange.

**Self-hosted deployments.** A deployment built in self-hosted mode does not run the forwarding
service, so it refuses a close that needs it. Withdraw to an address you control instead.

**If the forward is interrupted.** The failure screen and, on later visits with that account
connected, a banner reading **An exchange forward was interrupted** offer **Resume the forward**,
which delivers the balance with your memo. See
[When a step fails](/docs/users/when-a-step-fails#if-an-exchange-forward-was-interrupted).
